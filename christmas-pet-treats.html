CHRISTMAS WITH PETS

Christmas treats for dogs and cats: what’s safe, what’s not

Those big eyes under the dinner table are hard to resist. Here’s what you can share, what to keep well away, and how many treats is too many.

❌ Keep these away from pets

FoodWhy
Chocolate and cocoaToxic to dogs and cats. Treat every kind as off-limits, including baking chocolate.
Sweets and baking with xylitolXylitol (a sugar substitute in some sweets, gum, baking and peanut butter) can cause liver failure in dogs.
Grapes, raisins, currants, sultanasToxic to dogs. Hidden in fruitcake, mince pies, Christmas pudding and stollen.
Onions and garlicToxic to pets. Watch out for stuffing, gravy and leftovers.
Fatty skin, gravy and drippingsRich, fatty food can trigger pancreatitis, a painful and serious illness.
Cooked bonesCan splinter, cause choking or block the gut.
AlcoholKeep drinks out of reach, and remember that some desserts contain it.
Raw yeast doughCan cause painful bloating if swallowed.

The ASPCA suggests keeping your pet’s diet the same over the holidays, with a small piece of boneless turkey as the exception. Every pet is different, so if yours has a sensitive stomach or a health condition, check with your vet first.

How many treats is too many?

The World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) says treats should make up no more than 10% of a pet’s daily calories. Christmas extras add up fast, so here’s an easy way to stay on track:

  1. Find your pet’s daily calories with the FeedPetsWell calculator.
  2. Take 10% of that number. That’s the daily treat budget.
  3. On a treat-heavy day, serve a little less of the regular food.
Example: a dog who needs about 800 calories a day has a treat budget of about 80 calories. A cat who needs about 220 calories a day has about 22.

Christmas plants to watch

  • Lilies: highly toxic to cats and can cause kidney failure. Keep them out of any home with a cat.
  • Poinsettias: usually cause mild to moderate stomach upset.
  • Holly and mistletoe: can cause stomach upset and, with mistletoe, heart problems.
  • Amaryllis, pine, cedar and balsam can also be harmful (AVMA).
If you think your pet ate something harmful, call your vet or an emergency animal hospital right away. In Canada and the US you can also call the Pet Poison Helpline, 1-855-764-7661, or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center, 1-888-426-4435. A consultation fee may apply. Don’t wait for symptoms.

Questions people ask

Can my dog have turkey at Christmas?

A small piece of plain, boneless turkey with no skin, gravy or seasoning is generally fine for most dogs. Never give cooked bones, fatty skin or stuffing.

Can cats eat turkey?

A small bite of plain, cooked, boneless turkey is fine for most cats. Skip the skin, gravy and stuffing, which can contain onion and garlic.

How many treats can my pet have a day?

Treats should make up no more than 10% of your pet’s daily calories, according to WSAVA. Work out the daily total with the FeedPetsWell calculator, and 10% of that is the treat budget.

Is fruitcake dangerous for dogs?

Yes. Fruitcake, mince pies and Christmas pudding can contain raisins, currants or sultanas, which are toxic to dogs, and often alcohol too. Keep them well out of reach.

What if my dog ate chocolate?

Call your vet or a pet poison helpline straight away, even if your dog seems fine. Have the wrapper handy so you can say what kind and how much.
