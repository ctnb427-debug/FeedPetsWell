CHRISTMAS WITH PETS

Homemade Christmas treats for dogs and cats

The family is baking. Why should the pets miss out? These simple treats use a handful of pantry ingredients and make sweet stocking stuffers for the animal lovers on your list.

Before you start: check every label. Peanut butter must be xylitol-free, pumpkin must be plain purée, not pie filling, and never add chocolate, raisins, onion, garlic or sugar. If your pet has allergies or a health condition, ask your vet first.

FOR DOGS

🐕 Pumpkin peanut butter Christmas biscuits

  • Oven 350°F
  • 20 min prep
  • 20–25 min bake
  • Cut into stars, bones or trees

Ingredients

  • ⅔ cup plain pumpkin purée (not pie filling)
  • ¼ cup xylitol-free, unsalted peanut butter
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2½ to 3 cups whole wheat flour

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F and line a baking sheet with parchment.
  2. Beat the pumpkin, peanut butter and eggs together for 1 to 2 minutes.
  3. Mix in 2½ cups of flour, then add more, a little at a time, until the dough isn’t sticky.
  4. Roll out on a floured counter to about ¼ inch thick and cut out Christmas shapes.
  5. Bake 20 to 25 minutes, until the edges are golden. Cool completely before serving.

Recipe adapted from Zoetis Petcare. Small cookie cutters make treat-sized biscuits that are easier to fit into the daily treat budget.

FOR CATS

🐈 Tuna Christmas cat biscuits

  • Oven 180°C fan / 200°C
  • About 30 min bake
  • Makes about 25
  • 1 biscuit a day

Ingredients

  • 1 small can (145 g) tuna in spring water, drained
  • 120 g (about 1 cup) plain flour
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tbsp dried catnip (optional)

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180°C fan or 200°C regular (about 375 to 400°F).
  2. Blend everything into a thick, smooth dough.
  3. Roll into about 25 small balls and flatten slightly on a lined tray. Keep each one small.
  4. Bake about 30 minutes, until crisp. Cool before serving.
Serving size: one biscuit is about 10% of an average cat’s daily calories, so give one a day and serve a little less dinner that day. Keeps 3 days in an airtight container or a month in the freezer.

Recipe and serving advice from PDSA.

FOR DOGS (NO BAKING)

❄️ Frozen “snow day” lick mat

Spread a thin layer of plain pumpkin purée or your dog’s usual wet food on a lick mat and freeze for an hour or two. It keeps a dog busy and calm while guests arrive. Count it toward the day’s treats.

Wrap them up as gifts

Pack biscuits in a clean jar or a cellophane bag tied with ribbon, and add a little tag with the ingredients and the date. Pet-owning friends will love knowing exactly what’s inside.

How many is too many?

Homemade still counts! Treats should stay under 10% of your pet’s daily calories (WSAVA). Use the FeedPetsWell calculator to find your pet’s daily total, and see which Christmas foods are safe to share.

Questions people ask

Can I use regular peanut butter in dog treats?

Only if it has no xylitol (sometimes listed as birch sugar). Unsalted, plain peanut butter is best. Always read the label.

Can I use pumpkin pie filling?

No. Use plain 100% pumpkin purée. Pie filling has sugar and spices that dogs don’t need.

How long do homemade pet treats keep?

The tuna cat biscuits keep up to three days in an airtight container or a month in the freezer. Keep the dog biscuits in an airtight container, and freeze some if you’ve made a big batch.

Can cats eat the dog biscuits?

They’re made for dogs. Cats need different nutrition, so give cats their own treats, like the tuna biscuits.
