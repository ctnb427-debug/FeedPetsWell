CHRISTMAS WITH PETS

A calm Christmas for your pet

We love the noise, the guests and the twinkly lights. Our pets didn’t sign up for any of it. Here’s how to help your dog or cat feel safe from the first visitor to the last New Year’s firework.

Why Christmas is hard on pets

Pets love predictable days. December turns everything upside down: strangers at the door, late dinners, a tree in the living room, new smells, loud music and, to finish it off, fireworks. Even a confident pet can find it a lot, and a shy or rescue pet may find it frightening.

Signs your pet is stressed

🐕 Dogs

  • Panting or yawning a lot
  • Shaking, whining or barking
  • Pacing or hiding
  • Glancing nervously at the ceiling
  • Vomiting

🐈 Cats

  • Hiding
  • Eating or drinking less
  • Pacing or breathing heavily
  • Yowling or meowing more than usual
  • Looking visibly frightened

Signs from the RSPCA. A very frightened pet may also try to bolt, so watch the doors.

1. Set up a calm room before you need it

Choose a quiet room away from the party and let your pet get used to it now, not on Christmas Eve. Add their bed, water, favourite toys and, for cats, a litter tray and a box to hide in. Both the AVMA and the ASPCA recommend a quiet place pets can go to at any time.

Grandma’s trick: a lick mat spread with a little of their usual wet food and frozen gives a nervous pet something soothing to do while the doorbell rings.

2. Keep meals and walks on schedule

Feed at the usual times and serve the usual amount. Routine is comforting, and skipping a meal and then catching up later can upset tummies. Not sure how much is the right amount? The FeedPetsWell calculator gives you a daily estimate in seconds.

Take dogs out for a good long walk before guests arrive. A tired dog is a calmer dog.

3. Visitors: a little planning goes a long way

  • Tell guests about your pets before they come, and ask that nobody feeds them from the table.
  • Watch the door. Pets slip out easily during arrivals and goodbyes.
  • Check ID tags and microchip details are up to date, just in case.
  • Let your pet choose. No forced cuddles, especially from excited children.
  • Ask guests to keep medication zipped away in a closed cabinet, not in a handbag on the floor.
  • Secure the garbage. Turkey carcasses, bones and wrappers are a big temptation.

Based on advice from the AVMA and the ASPCA.

4. Decorate with pets in mind

  • Anchor the tree so it can’t tip, and keep pets away from the tree water.
  • Skip the tinsel. Cats especially love it, and swallowed tinsel can block the intestines.
  • Keep ribbons, ornaments, light cords and batteries out of reach. Salt-dough ornaments are dangerous even when dry.
  • Never leave candles burning with pets alone in the room.
  • Choose plants carefully. Lilies can cause kidney failure in cats. Poinsettias, holly and mistletoe can upset stomachs or worse. Artificial is safest.

More on food and plants in our guide to what pets can and can’t eat at Christmas.

5. Travelling with your pet

Use a secure carrier or a harness so your pet is restrained in the car, and never leave them alone in a vehicle. Pack their usual food, any medication, their records and ID. Crossing a border may need a health certificate from your vet, so check the rules early (AVMA).

6. New Year’s Eve fireworks

Before the bangs

  • Walk dogs in daylight
  • Keep cats indoors after dark and close the cat flap
  • Settle them in their calm room early
  • Close curtains and windows
  • Ask your vet about pheromone diffusers or medication, weeks ahead

During the bangs

  • Play music or the TV to mask the noise
  • Let them hide; don’t pull them out
  • Stay close and act calm and normal
  • Never shout or punish
  • Don’t take dogs to fireworks displays

Advice from the RSPCA, which also suggests gradual sound training for dogs, started well in advance.

If you think your pet ate something harmful, call your vet or an emergency animal hospital right away. In Canada and the US you can also call the Pet Poison Helpline, 1-855-764-7661, or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center, 1-888-426-4435. A consultation fee may apply. Don’t wait for symptoms.

Questions people ask

Should I comfort my dog when the fireworks start?

Yes, stay close and act calm and normal. The RSPCA advises letting a frightened pet hide or pace without coaxing them out, staying nearby, and never shouting at or punishing them, because that makes fear worse.

Is it okay to put my cat in a room on her own during a party?

Yes. A quiet room with her litter tray, water, a bed and a hiding spot is often the kindest choice. Close the door so guests don’t wander in, and check on her now and then.

Can calming products help?

Some pets do well with pheromone diffusers, which work best when started weeks ahead. Anxiety medication is also an option for very frightened pets. Both are worth discussing with your vet well before New Year’s Eve.

My pet is off their food over the holidays. Should I worry?

A small dip in appetite during a busy day can happen, but a pet who stops eating, hides for long periods, vomits or has diarrhea should see a vet. Cats in particular shouldn’t go without food.
